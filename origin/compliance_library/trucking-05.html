1.0 Purpose

This program establishes the requirements of {{COMPANY NAME}} to ensure that all cargo is properly distributed, secured, and restrained so that it does not shift, leak, or fall during transport. {{COMPANY NAME}} shall comply with the cargo securement rules of 49 CFR Part 393, Subpart I.

2.0 Scope & Application

This program applies to all {{COMPANY NAME}} drivers and loaders who load, secure, or transport cargo on commercial motor vehicles, including flatbed, hotshot, and oilfield equipment hauling.

3.0 Definitions

Working Load Limit (WLL) — the maximum load that a securement device may hold, as marked or assigned under 49 CFR 393.108.

Tiedown — a securement device (chain, strap, binder) used to attach cargo to the vehicle.

Aggregate Working Load Limit — the sum of the WLLs of all securement devices used, which must be at least one-half the cargo weight per 49 CFR 393.106.

4.0 Roles & Responsibilities

▪ Safety Manager: shall ensure drivers are trained in securement, that adequate securement devices are provided, and that damaged devices are removed from service.

▪ Drivers: shall secure cargo before transport, inspect securement within the first 50 miles and at required intervals, and re-secure as needed, per 49 CFR 392.9.

5.0 Program Requirements

General Securement

▪ Cargo shall be firmly immobilized or secured on or within the vehicle by structures of adequate strength, dunnage, tiedowns, or a combination, per 49 CFR 393.100.

▪ The aggregate working load limit of tiedowns shall be at least one-half the weight of the cargo, per 49 CFR 393.106.

▪ The number of tiedowns shall meet the minimums of 49 CFR 393.110 based on cargo length and weight.

Commodity-Specific Rules

▪ Cargo covered by the commodity-specific rules of 49 CFR 393.116–393.136 (e.g., logs, metal coils, pipe, machinery, vehicles) shall be secured in accordance with those rules.

▪ Oilfield tubulars, machinery, and heavy equipment shall be secured to the applicable commodity-specific standard.

Inspection En Route

▪ The driver shall inspect the cargo and securement within the first 50 miles and re-secure as necessary, per 49 CFR 392.9.

▪ Securement shall be re-examined at each change of duty status or every 3 hours / 150 miles, whichever occurs first.

6.0 Training

▪ Drivers and loaders shall be trained on securement selection, working load limits, tiedown counts, and commodity-specific requirements.

▪ Training shall be refreshed periodically and whenever new cargo types are handled.

7.0 Recordkeeping

▪ Training records and securement-device inspection records shall be maintained and made available upon request.

8.0 Regulatory References

▪ 49 CFR Part 393, Subpart I — Protection Against Shifting and Falling Cargo

▪ 49 CFR 392.9 — Inspection of Cargo and Securement Devices
