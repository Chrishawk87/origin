1.0 Purpose

This program establishes the requirements of {{COMPANY NAME}} to protect employees from electrical hazards during construction work. {{COMPANY NAME}} shall comply with 29 CFR 1926 Subpart K and, where applicable, NFPA 70E for arc-flash and shock protection.

2.0 Scope & Application

This program applies to all {{COMPANY NAME}} employees who may be exposed to electrical hazards, including work near energized parts, temporary wiring, extension cords, and power tools on construction sites.

3.0 Definitions

Qualified Person — one who has received training in and has demonstrated skills and knowledge in the construction and operation of electrical equipment and the hazards involved, per 29 CFR 1926.32(m).

GFCI — Ground-Fault Circuit Interrupter used to protect employees from ground faults, per 29 CFR 1926.404(b).

Assured Equipment Grounding Conductor Program — a documented program to ensure grounding of cord sets and equipment, per 29 CFR 1926.404(b)(1)(iii).

4.0 Roles & Responsibilities

▪ Competent/Qualified Person: shall assess electrical hazards, ensure only qualified persons work on or near energized parts, and verify grounding and GFCI protection.

▪ Supervisors: shall ensure de-energization and lockout where feasible and that damaged equipment is removed from service.

▪ Employees: shall inspect cords and tools before use, use GFCI protection, and report electrical hazards.

5.0 Program Requirements

Ground-Fault Protection

▪ {{COMPANY NAME}} shall protect employees using GFCIs on 120-volt, single-phase, 15- and 20-ampere receptacles, or shall implement an assured equipment grounding conductor program, per 29 CFR 1926.404(b)(1).

▪ Flexible cords and tools shall be inspected before use and removed from service if defective.

Working On or Near Energized Parts

▪ Only qualified persons shall work on or near exposed energized parts, per 29 CFR 1926.416.

▪ Equipment shall be de-energized and locked/tagged out before employees work on it unless de-energizing introduces greater hazard or is infeasible.

▪ Where arc-flash or shock hazards exist, protective approach boundaries and appropriate PPE (per NFPA 70E) shall be observed.

Overhead Lines & Clearances

▪ Employees and equipment shall maintain the minimum clearances from overhead power lines required by 29 CFR 1926.1408 and 1926.416; lines shall be treated as energized unless confirmed de-energized and grounded.

6.0 Training

▪ Employees shall be trained to recognize and avoid electrical hazards; qualified persons shall receive additional training per 29 CFR 1926.21 and Subpart K.

▪ Retraining shall be provided when work practices or equipment change.

7.0 Recordkeeping

▪ The assured equipment grounding conductor program records and inspection logs shall be maintained.

▪ Training records shall be retained and available upon request.

8.0 Regulatory References

▪ 29 CFR 1926 Subpart K — Electrical (1926.400–449)

▪ NFPA 70E — Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace (arc-flash/shock)
