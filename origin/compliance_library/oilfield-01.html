1.0 Purpose

This Hazard Communication Program establishes the requirements of {{COMPANY NAME}} to ensure that the hazards of all chemicals used, produced, or brought onto our worksites are evaluated and that this information is communicated to employees and affected personnel. {{COMPANY NAME}} shall comply with the OSHA Hazard Communication Standard, 29 CFR 1910.1200, and the Globally Harmonized System (GHS) of classification and labeling.

2.0 Scope & Application

This program applies to all {{COMPANY NAME}} employees, field crews, and operations where employees may be exposed to hazardous chemicals under normal conditions or in a foreseeable emergency. All personnel shall comply with this program and with the host operator's site-specific chemical requirements.

3.0 Definitions

Hazardous Chemical — any chemical classified as a physical or health hazard, a simple asphyxiant, combustible dust, or pyrophoric gas.

Safety Data Sheet (SDS) — a standardized 16-section document providing information on a chemical's hazards and safe handling.

GHS — the Globally Harmonized System of Classification and Labelling of Chemicals.

Pictogram — a GHS symbol on a label conveying specific hazard information.

4.0 Roles & Responsibilities

▪ Program Administrator: shall maintain the written program, the chemical inventory, and the SDS library, and shall ensure labels and training remain current.

▪ Supervisors: shall ensure employees are trained before working with hazardous chemicals and that all containers on the job are properly labeled.

▪ Employees: shall follow safe-handling procedures, use required PPE, and immediately report unlabeled containers or missing SDSs.

5.0 Program Requirements

Chemical Inventory

▪ {{COMPANY NAME}} shall maintain a written inventory of all hazardous chemicals present at its worksites.

▪ The inventory shall be updated whenever a new chemical is introduced and reviewed at least annually.

▪ Each listed chemical shall correspond to a current Safety Data Sheet.

Safety Data Sheets (SDS)

▪ A current SDS shall be maintained and readily accessible to employees for each hazardous chemical, on every shift and at each jobsite where the chemical is present.

▪ Employees shall be informed of the location of the SDS library and shall have access without barrier.

Labels & Warnings

▪ All containers of hazardous chemicals shall be labeled with the product identifier and appropriate hazard warnings; labels shall not be removed or defaced.

▪ Secondary and workplace containers shall be labeled in accordance with 29 CFR 1910.1200(f).

Non-Routine Tasks & Multi-Employer Sites

▪ Employees shall be informed of the hazards of non-routine tasks before performing them.

▪ {{COMPANY NAME}} shall exchange SDS and hazard information with on-site contractors and the host operator on multi-employer worksites.

6.0 Training

▪ Employees shall be trained at initial assignment and whenever a new chemical hazard is introduced to their work area.

▪ Training shall cover this program, the standard, methods to detect the presence or release of a hazardous chemical, physical and health hazards, protective measures, and how to read labels and SDSs.

7.0 Recordkeeping

▪ The written program, chemical inventory, and SDS library shall be retained and made available to employees and regulators upon request.

▪ Training records shall be maintained per company policy and host-client requirements.

8.0 Regulatory References

▪ 29 CFR 1910.1200 — Hazard Communication

▪ Globally Harmonized System (GHS) — labeling and SDS format
