1.0 Purpose

This program establishes the requirements of {{COMPANY NAME}} to protect employees from cave-ins and other hazards associated with excavation and trenching work. {{COMPANY NAME}} shall comply with 29 CFR 1926 Subpart P.

2.0 Scope & Application

This program applies to all {{COMPANY NAME}} excavation and trenching operations, including any man-made cut, cavity, trench, or depression formed by earth removal, and to all employees who enter or work near excavations.

3.0 Definitions

Excavation — any man-made cut, cavity, trench, or depression in an earth surface formed by earth removal.

Protective System — a method of protecting employees from cave-ins, such as sloping, benching, shoring, or shielding (trench box).

Competent Person — one capable of identifying existing and predictable hazards and authorized to take prompt corrective measures, per 29 CFR 1926.650.

Registered Professional Engineer — a person registered as a professional engineer who approves designs where required by Subpart P.

4.0 Roles & Responsibilities

▪ Competent Person: shall classify the soil, select and inspect the protective system, conduct daily and as-needed inspections, and remove employees when hazards exist.

▪ Supervisors: shall ensure a protective system is in place before entry and that utilities are located.

▪ Employees: shall not enter an unprotected excavation and shall follow entry/exit and spoil-placement requirements.

5.0 Program Requirements

Cave-In Protection

▪ Each employee in an excavation shall be protected from cave-ins by an adequate protective system, per 29 CFR 1926.652, except where excavations are made entirely in stable rock or are less than 5 feet deep and examined to present no hazard.

▪ Protective systems shall be selected based on soil classification, depth, and site conditions in accordance with 1926.652 and its appendices.

Access, Utilities & Hazardous Atmospheres

▪ A stairway, ladder, ramp, or other safe means of egress shall be provided in trenches 4 feet or more deep, within 25 feet of employees, per 29 CFR 1926.651(c).

▪ Underground utilities shall be located and marked before excavation; spoil and equipment shall be kept at least 2 feet from the edge.

▪ Where a hazardous atmosphere exists or could reasonably exist in excavations more than 4 feet deep, the atmosphere shall be tested and controlled, per 29 CFR 1926.651(g).

Inspection

▪ A competent person shall inspect excavations, adjacent areas, and protective systems daily before work begins, as needed throughout the shift, and after every rainstorm or hazard-increasing occurrence, per 29 CFR 1926.651(k).

▪ Where evidence of a possible cave-in or hazard exists, employees shall be removed until precautions are taken.

6.0 Training

▪ Competent persons shall be trained to classify soil, select protective systems, and conduct inspections.

▪ Employees shall be trained on excavation hazards, safe entry/exit, and emergency procedures.

7.0 Recordkeeping

▪ Soil classification, inspection findings, and any engineered designs shall be documented and retained.

▪ Training records shall be maintained and available upon request.

8.0 Regulatory References

▪ 29 CFR 1926 Subpart P — Excavations (1926.650–652)
