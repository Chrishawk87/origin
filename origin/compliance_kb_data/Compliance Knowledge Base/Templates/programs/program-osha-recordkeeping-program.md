# OSHA Injury & Illness Recordkeeping Program

## 3. Determining Work-Relatedness *(1904.5)*
A case is work-related if an event or exposure in the work environment caused, contributed to, or significantly aggravated the condition. Work-relatedness is **presumed** unless one of the exceptions in 1904.5(b)(2) applies (e.g., present as a member of the public; symptoms surface at work but result solely from a non-work event; voluntary wellness activity; eating/drinking own food; personal grooming; common cold or flu). Travel-status and work-at-home cases follow the special rules in 1904.5(b)(6)-(7). The basis for any "not work-related" decision is documented.

## 4. Recordability Decision *(1904.7)*
Once a case is work-related and a new case, it is **recordable** if it results in any of: death; days away from work; restricted work or job transfer; medical treatment beyond first aid; loss of consciousness; or a significant injury/illness diagnosed by a physician or licensed health care professional.

- The **first-aid list** in 1904.7(b)(5)(ii) is exhaustive — any treatment **not** on that list is "medical treatment beyond first aid" and is recordable (e.g., prescription medication, sutures, rigid splints).
- Day counts use **calendar days**, begin the day **after** the injury, and are **capped at 180 days**.
- If a case worsens (restricted → days away), reclassify to the most serious outcome.
- Apply the special criteria (1904.8-.12) for needlesticks/sharps, medical removal, and hearing loss (STS plus a 25 dB level).

## 5. The Forms *(1904.29)*
- **OSHA 300 Log:** each recordable case entered within **7 calendar days** of learning of it.
- **OSHA 301 Incident Report** (or an equivalent such as a workers' comp first report): completed within **7 calendar days**.
- **Privacy concern cases:** do **not** put the employee's name on the 300 Log — enter "privacy case" and keep a **separate confidential list**. Privacy cases include injuries to intimate body parts/reproductive system, sexual assaults, mental illnesses, HIV/hepatitis/TB, contaminated needlesticks, and any case where the employee voluntarily requests name omission.

[[State where the 300 Log, 301s, and the confidential privacy list are kept, and who has access.]]

## 6. Annual Summary — OSHA 300A *(1904.32)*
At year end the recordkeeper reviews the 300 Log for accuracy, totals the columns, and completes the 300A including average number of employees and total hours worked. A **company executive** — an owner, an officer, the highest-ranking company official at the site, or that person's immediate supervisor — certifies the 300A. It is posted in a conspicuous place from **February 1 through April 30** of the following year, **even if there were zero recordable cases**.

## 7. Reporting Fatalities and Severe Injuries *(1904.39)*
Regardless of size or partial-exemption status, {{COMPANY_NAME}} reports:

- a work-related **fatality within 8 hours**;
- a work-related **in-patient hospitalization, amputation, or loss of an eye within 24 hours**.

Reports are made via OSHA's 800-number, the online form, or the nearest area office. A record of each report (date, time, who reported, confirmation) is kept.

## 8. Electronic Submission — ITA *(1904.41)*
{{COMPANY_NAME}} determines each establishment's ITA obligation by size and industry:

- 250+ employees (in recordkeeping industries): submit the **300A**.
- 20-249 employees in designated higher-hazard industries (Appendix A): submit the **300A**.
- 100+ employees in the highest-hazard industries (Appendix B, per the 2024 rule): submit the **300 Log and 301** data in addition to the 300A, including the legal company name.

The annual deadline is **March 2** for the prior calendar year. Submitted totals are reconciled to the posted 300A.

## 9. Retention, Updating, and Access *(1904.33, .35, .40)*
The 300 Log, the privacy-case list, the 300A, and the 301 Reports are retained for **5 years** following the end of the calendar year they cover. During that period the stored 300 Log is **updated** to add newly discovered recordable cases and to reflect changes. Employees and their representatives are given access to their records (1904.35); government requests are met within **4 business hours** (1904.40).
