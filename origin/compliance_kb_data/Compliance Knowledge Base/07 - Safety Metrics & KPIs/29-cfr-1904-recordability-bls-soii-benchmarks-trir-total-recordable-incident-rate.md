# TRIR — Total Recordable Incident Rate

**Citation:** 29 CFR 1904 (recordability) / BLS SOII (benchmarks)
**Category:** 07 - Safety Metrics & KPIs
**Written program required:** Reference
**Agencies commonly requiring (Y=commonly, C=client/scope-driven):** ISN: Y, Avetta: Y, Veriforce: Y, PEC: Y
**Related citations:** 29 CFR 1904.7, 29 CFR 1904.32 (300A)

## Applicability / Trigger

The headline OSHA rate: all recordable injuries and illnesses per 100 full-time-equivalent workers per year. Used by every prequalification platform and most GCs as a first-pass safety screen. Computed from the OSHA 300 log and total hours worked.

## Formula

TRIR = ( Number of OSHA-recordable cases × 200,000 ) ÷ Total hours worked

## Worked Example

8 recordable cases over 430,000 hours worked → (8 × 200,000) ÷ 430,000 = 3.72 TRIR.

## Required Elements / Components

- Count of OSHA-recordable cases (any case meeting 1904.7: death, days away, restricted/transfer, medical treatment beyond first aid, loss of consciousness, or significant diagnosis)
- Total hours actually worked by all employees in the period (do NOT use 2,000×headcount if actual hours are available; exclude vacation/leave)
- 200,000-hour base constant (100 employees × 40 hrs × 50 weeks)

## Benchmarks / Standards

BLS Survey of Occupational Injuries & Illnesses (SOII), 2024 data (released Jan 2026): all private industry ≈ 2.3–2.7; Construction (NAICS 23) ≈ 2.3–2.5; Oil & gas extraction (NAICS 211) ≈ 0.8–1.4; Support activities for O&G (213112) ≈ 1.6; Manufacturing ≈ 2.8–3.2; Transportation & warehousing ≈ 4.0–4.8. Rates update annually — always pull the CURRENT BLS figure for the contractor's specific NAICS. Many GCs set a hard bid cutoff of TRIR < 1.0–2.0.

## Common Failure Points (why reviewers reject it)

- Using scheduled hours instead of hours actually worked
- Counting first-aid-only cases as recordable (over-counting) OR omitting recordable restricted-duty cases (under-counting)
- Rate that doesn't reconcile to the submitted OSHA 300A totals
- Three-year trend inconsistent with the 300A logs on file

## Authoritative Source

https://www.osha.gov/legacy/injuryreporting

## Notes for the Agent

TRIR is self-reported but must tie exactly to the OSHA 300A logs the contractor uploads. Platforms score the 3-year TRIR against the NAICS benchmark; a rate above the industry average with no improvement trend is a common grade downgrade.
