# DART — Days Away, Restricted, or Transferred Rate

**Citation:** 29 CFR 1904.7 / BLS SOII (benchmarks)
**Category:** 07 - Safety Metrics & KPIs
**Written program required:** Reference
**Agencies commonly requiring (Y=commonly, C=client/scope-driven):** ISN: Y, Avetta: Y, Veriforce: Y, PEC: Y

## Applicability / Trigger

A severity-weighted rate: only the more serious cases — those resulting in days away from work, restricted duty, or job transfer — per 100 FTE. Always ≤ TRIR (it is a subset). Distinguishes serious injuries from minor medical-treatment cases.

## Formula

DART = ( Number of DART cases × 200,000 ) ÷ Total hours worked

## Worked Example

3 DART cases over 430,000 hours → (3 × 200,000) ÷ 430,000 = 1.40 DART.

## Required Elements / Components

- Count of DART cases (cases with days away, restricted work, or job transfer per the 300 log columns)
- Total hours worked
- 200,000 base constant

## Benchmarks / Standards

BLS SOII 2024: all private industry DART ≈ 1.4–1.5; Construction ≈ 1.5; Oil & gas extraction ≈ 0.4–0.9; Manufacturing ≈ 1.6–1.8. DART is roughly 55–65% of TRIR in most industries.

## Common Failure Points (why reviewers reject it)

- Confusing DART with DAFW/LTIR — DART includes restricted/transfer, lost-time (DAFW) does not
- Not classifying a restricted-duty case as DART because 'no days were lost'
- DART higher than TRIR (impossible — a data error)

## Notes for the Agent

Return-to-work / light-duty programs are the primary DART lever: keeping an injured worker on restricted duty still counts as a DART case, but converting a would-be lost-time case to medical-only keeps it OUT of DART. This is also why aggressive RTW lowers EMR.
