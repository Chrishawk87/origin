# Fatality Rate & OSHA Reporting Triggers

**Citation:** 29 CFR 1904.39 / BLS CFOI (benchmarks)
**Category:** 07 - Safety Metrics & KPIs
**Written program required:** Reference
**Agencies commonly requiring (Y=commonly, C=client/scope-driven):** ISN: Y, Avetta: Y, Veriforce: Y, PEC: Y
**Related citations:** 29 CFR 1904.39

## Applicability / Trigger

Fatalities are reported to OSHA directly (not just logged) and, for benchmarking, expressed per 100,000 FTE from the BLS Census of Fatal Occupational Injuries (CFOI). Critical because fatality/severe-injury reporting failures are their own citation and a prequalification red flag.

## Formula

Fatality Rate = ( Number of fatalities × 200,000 ) ÷ Total hours worked  (per-100-FTE basis)
BLS CFOI reports fatal injuries per 100,000 full-time-equivalent workers.

## Required Elements / Components

- Report any work-related fatality to OSHA within 8 hours
- Report any in-patient hospitalization, amputation, or loss of an eye within 24 hours
- Report by phone (1-800-321-OSHA), the nearest area office, or the online reporting form
- Log the fatality on the OSHA 300 and retain the incident investigation

## Benchmarks / Standards

BLS CFOI: all-worker fatal injury rate ≈ 3.5 per 100,000 FTE; Construction ≈ 9.6; Oil & gas / mining ≈ 5–11; Agriculture ≈ 18–20; Transportation ≈ 13–14.

## Common Failure Points (why reviewers reject it)

- Missing the 8-hour fatality / 24-hour hospitalization-amputation-eye reporting window
- Reporting to the wrong channel or not documenting the report
- No incident investigation on file to accompany the report

## Notes for the Agent

Even one late severe-injury report can trigger an inspection and damage a prequalification score. The 8-hour / 24-hour rule is a frequent audit question.
