# Recording and Reporting Occupational Injuries and Illnesses

**Citation:** 29 CFR 1904
**Category:** 01 - General Industry (29 CFR 1910)
**Written program required:** Reference
**Agencies commonly requiring (Y=commonly, C=client/scope-driven):** ISN: Y, Avetta: Y, Veriforce: Y, PEC: Y

## Applicability / Trigger

Applies to most employers with more than 10 employees (unless in a partially exempt low-hazard industry). Underpins the TRIR/DART metrics prequal platforms grade.

## Required Written-Program Elements

- OSHA 300 Log of work-related injuries and illnesses
- OSHA 301 Incident Report (or equivalent) within 7 calendar days
- OSHA 300A Annual Summary — posted Feb 1 to Apr 30, certified by a company executive
- Severe-injury reporting: fatality within 8 hours; in-patient hospitalization, amputation, or eye loss within 24 hours
- Electronic submission (300A, and 300/301 for certain establishments) via ITA where required

## Recordkeeping & Retention

Retain OSHA 300, 300A, and 301 forms for 5 years following the year they cover.

## Common Failure Points (why reviewers reject it)

- 300A not certified by a company executive
- Late severe-injury reporting
- Recordability decisions inconsistent (drives inflated/deflated TRIR)
